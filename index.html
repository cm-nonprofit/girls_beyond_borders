Project Firefly · Myanmar Girls Education Initiative

Find the girls. Understand the barriers. Build a path back to school.

Across rural and ethnic minority communities in Myanmar, girls leave school for reasons that are often invisible. We go into communities to find them, one girl and one barrier at a time.

See our approach Support a girl's return
The Challenge

A girl can leave school for many reasons.

There is rarely a single reason why a girl stops going to school, and traditional aid often treats every girl as if she faces the same problem.

  • For one girl, school may simply be too far away.
  • Another spends her day caring for younger siblings.
  • Another struggles because the language at home differs from the language in class.
  • Some face pressure to marry early.
  • Others miss school without safe, private sanitation or adolescent health education.

We believe every girl deserves to be understood individually.

The Girl Education Risk Index

At the center of Project Firefly is a seven-dimension framework designed to identify the factors that place a girl at risk of leaving school.

The goal is not simply to collect data. The goal is to turn data into action.

Our Approach

From general aid to precision educational support.

Girls Beyond Borders started as a student project and grew through fieldwork in monastery and nunnery schools and in family homes in Myanmar. That experience became Project Firefly, which combines community engagement, field research, technology and long-term educational support around one simple principle.

FIND

Identify out-of-school and high-risk girls through household surveys and community outreach.

UNDERSTAND

Evaluate the specific educational barriers affecting each girl.

ACT

Develop individualized interventions based on what each girl actually needs.

TRACK

Follow outcomes over time, not just the first day back in class.

SCALE

Use anonymized pilot evidence to improve the model and reach new communities.

Programs

One girl. One barrier. One solution.

Not every out-of-school girl needs the same kind of help. Each program targets a specific part of the path back to school, and a path to staying there.

Village Education Map

Household surveys combined with GPS mapping. Each girl is represented by an anonymous ID to protect her privacy. The map shows where exclusion is concentrated and where limited resources can do the most.

  • Villages, schools and out-of-school girls
  • High-risk girls and local risk factors
  • A Girls Education Dashboard

Village Education Fellows

Trained local volunteers, with priority given to young women and local high school graduates, who conduct surveys, interview families, build individual action plans and follow each girl after she returns to school.

  • Community conversations and parent engagement
  • Female role-model sharing

Keeping Girls in School

Returning to school is only the beginning. We look at the everyday conditions that quietly push girls back out, then work with schools to fix them.

  • Separate restrooms with working locks
  • Clean water and adequate privacy
  • Menstrual and adolescent health education

Girls Learning Hubs

Community spaces that offer English, mathematics, digital literacy and tutoring. U.S. student clubs can contribute recorded lessons, and content is prepared in advance wherever internet access is limited.

  • Offline-ready lessons
  • Remote academic support from U.S. schools

Girl Digital Leaders

Moving from digital access to digital empowerment, with the aim of building the knowledge, confidence and skills girls need to lead in their own communities.

  • Computer basics and office software
  • Internet research and digital literacy
  • Responsible use of AI tools

Individual Action Plans

Targeted support matched to each girl's barrier, whether that means a bicycle, a scholarship, language tutoring, family engagement or community advocacy against early marriage.

Lives far from schoolA bicycle
Financial hardshipEducational support or a scholarship
Struggles with the classroom languageLanguage tutoring
Heavy household responsibilitiesFamily engagement
At risk of early marriageCommunity-based intervention and advocacy

We do not ask, “What can we give?”
We ask, “What is preventing this girl from learning?”

About Us · A Message from the Founder

Every girl seen. Every barrier understood.

“The education of rural girls in Myanmar is a complex social issue. Short-term material donations alone cannot break cycles of school dropout and early marriage.

I believe we must first understand the difficulties facing each individual girl, use data to identify patterns that might otherwise remain invisible, and work alongside local communities to create solutions that can last.

Education should do more than bring a girl back into a classroom. It should give her the knowledge, confidence and skills to shape her own future — and, one day, help shape the future of her community.”

Olivia Huo (Huo Yutong)Founder, Girls Beyond Borders
